What changed in this experiment?

This experiment retains the body poses estimated by GVHMR and adjusts whole-body position to improve contact. Kimodo does not regenerate the motion. These adjustments can reduce some intersections, but may lift the person off the ground and leave an incorrect crouching pose unchanged.

The adjusted reconstruction is an experimental result, not a claim that the issues are resolved. Failed results remain available for full playback.

How does the workflow work, and where does GPT-6 participate?

Models and scripts perform the computation. GPT-6 reviews the images, establishes reconstruction scope and assesses the results.

StepScript operationsGPT-6 review and decisions
1. Select the person and clipSAM3 tracks and segments the selected person across framesConfirm identity, detect identity switches and distinguish occlusion from leaving the frame
2. Reconstruct the roomPi3X estimates depth and cameras; Blender builds the room from the reviewed parametersAssess floor, wall and furniture placement, and compare the room with the video
3. Estimate body motionViTPose detects joints; GVHMR estimates body poses and global motionCompare motion with the source and flag occlusions, incorrect poses and unreliable intervals
4. Place the person and refine requested contactsUse fixed whole-clip alignment by default, preserving body scale and trajectory shape; this contact experiment solves additional correctionsIdentify the target object, contact times and body parts; assess whether Kimodo motion completion is needed
5. Validate and presentCheck intersections, floating and foot slip; render the full video and verify its durationCompare contact and motion with the source and label failures

Once the person, scene and contact intervals are established, scripts can repeat the experiment without GPT-6 reviewing every frame. New people, scenes, occlusions or unexpected results require visual review.
